Planet Sim: building Earth one level at a time

This is my personal project: a simplified model of how a planet like Earth is put together, using real Earth numbers. Each level adds one new piece of physics and answers one question. Level 1 asks: what's inside? AI used: built with an AI coding agent. It fills the globe with the standard model of Earth's interior (PREM) and checks it against Earth's real mass, gravity and moment of inertia.

How to use it: drag to spin the globe and scroll to zoom. Flip the Cutaway switch to slice it open, then hover over the slice to read the depth and density. Below, try building your own 4-shell Earth.

How we know

No one has drilled deeper than about \(12\,\mathrm{km}\), so the inside is measured indirectly. Earthquakes send seismic waves through the planet. Their travel times to stations around the world give wave speeds at each depth, and sudden jumps show where layers meet. S waves do not travel through the liquid outer core; together with P-wave refraction, this helps reveal its boundary. The density has to fit those speeds, and also add up to Earth's total mass (known from gravity and orbits) and its moment of inertia (known from how Earth's spin axis precesses). PREM (Dziewonski & Anderson, 1981) is the model that fits all of these at once.

Why start with density?

Once we know how mass is arranged inside, a lot follows from it:

  • Gravity at every depth, from the mass below you (the \(g\) plot above): \(g(r) = G M(r) / r^{2}\).
  • Pressure, from the weight of the rock above you: \(\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}r = -\rho g\), with \(P = 0\) at the surface. Pressure and temperature together determine which minerals are stable, for example the \(410\) and \(660\,\mathrm{km}\) boundaries are mineral phase changes.
  • Moment of inertia: a planet with its mass packed toward the center spins and wobbles differently (\(I / MR^{2}\) is smaller when mass is central).
  • Buoyancy: density contrasts create buoyancy (density alone does not predict motion). In Level 2, rigid plates ride on the mantle. In Level 3, isostasy explains why oceans sit low and continents stand high.

So density is the foundation. Every later level builds on this one.

What Level 1 assumes

  • Perfect sphere (real Earth is flattened \(\sim 1/298\) by spin).
  • Density depends only on radius (PREM is a 1D average; real Earth has slabs, plumes, continents).
  • Same radial profile under every hex cell.
  • Static snapshot: nothing moves or evolves.
  • Gravity from the shell theorem, \(g = G M(r) / r^{2}\).
  • Pressure is hydrostatic: \(\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}r = -\rho g\), \(P = 0\) at the surface.
  • The 4-shell model uses uniform shells.
  • Hex cells drawn with flat faces (fine at \(\sim 220\,\mathrm{km}\) cell size).
